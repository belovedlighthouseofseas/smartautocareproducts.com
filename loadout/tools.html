Tools.

The metal and plastic between your hand and the paint. Foam cannons, pump sprayers, brushes, applicators — the hardware that determines whether a product performs.

What this lane covers

You can have the right product and still get the wrong result if the tool is wrong. A cheap foam cannon delivers thin foam that runs off before it does anything. A wrong applicator pad smears polish across the panel instead of working it in. Tools matter as much as chemistry.

The categories that earn their place on the cart:

  • Foam cannons & lances — for pre-wash foam soaking. Quality matters here; cheap ones don't produce dense foam.
  • Pump sprayers — for distributing rinseless wash, IPA wipes, interior cleaners, water-based dressings.
  • Detailing brushes — graded by stiffness for different surfaces. Soft for interior trim and badges, medium for tires, stiff for wheel wells.
  • Applicators — foam blocks, microfiber suedes, leather pads. Each compound, polish, or coating has a preferred applicator type.
  • Spray bottles & triggers — chemical-resistant, calibrated triggers. Refill 16-oz size for portability; bulk gallons for refill stations.
  • Buckets, grit guards, drying tools — the unsexy hardware that prevents marring.

How to pick

Driveway owner: a foam cannon attachment for your pressure washer is the single biggest upgrade. Add one quality pump sprayer for rinseless or interior.

Mobile detailer: brass quick-connect foam cannon, two pump sprayers (one for IPA, one for interior), and a graded brush set.

Shop: bulk applicator pads, multiple pump sprayers per station, and replace consumables (foam blocks, brushes) on a schedule before they degrade.

Shop note

Label your sprayers. Mixing up a wheel cleaner sprayer with an interior glass cleaner sprayer ruins both products and possibly the surface. Color-coded triggers help.

Common items quoted in this lane

  • Detailing brush sets (graded stiffness)
