Buying microfiber without getting ripped off.

GSM, blend ratios, edge construction. The three numbers that matter — and the marketing terms that don't.

Read ~5 min Lane Bulk Restock For Everyone

The three numbers that matter

Most microfiber marketing is noise. Ignore the brand name, the color, the "premium" badge. Look for three things:

  • GSM (grams per square meter) — the weight/density of the towel. Higher GSM = plusher, more absorbent, generally gentler on paint. Common ranges: 300 GSM (utility), 400-500 GSM (general detailing), 600+ GSM (drying, final wipe, ceramic application).
  • Blend ratio — usually 70/30 or 80/20 polyester/polyamide. Higher polyamide = softer, more absorbent, more expensive. 80/20 is the modern detail-grade standard.
  • Edge construction — sewn-edge, silk-banded, edgeless. The edge is where most marring comes from. Silk-banded and edgeless are paint-safe; cheap sewn edges with hard polyester thread can scratch.

If a product page won't tell you these three numbers, that's information.

Match the towel to the job

You don't need one perfect towel — you need different towels for different stages. A 600 GSM plush is wasted on tire dressing. A 300 GSM utility towel will ruin a freshly polished panel.

A working grade-out:

  • 300-350 GSM, edgeless utility: wheels, tires, engine bay, glass.
  • 400-500 GSM, silk-banded: interior trim, exterior dressing, general work.
  • 600+ GSM, plush: drying, final wipe after wax/sealant, ceramic application.
  • Microfiber suede (no nap): ceramic application only. Different texture entirely.

The marketing terms to ignore

"Korean microfiber" — used to mean something in 2010. Now it's a marketing label. Korean factories make great and terrible microfiber, same as anywhere.

"Detailer-grade" — meaningless. There's no certification body.

"Streak-free" — every microfiber is streak-free if you're using it correctly. If it's streaking, you're using it too dry or it's saturated.

Specific color = specific quality — color is just dye. Plenty of shops color-code by use (yellow for interior, blue for paint, etc.) which is smart, but the color itself isn't a quality signal.

Wash discipline matters more than brand

A premium 600 GSM towel washed with regular detergent and fabric softener becomes a marring towel in three washes. The fabric softener coats the fibers and reduces absorbency; regular detergent residue causes streaking.

Use a dedicated microfiber detergent (or just clean unscented liquid). Skip fabric softener entirely. Wash hot, dry low (or hang). Replace towels when they start feeling rough on your skin.

Buying in bulk

Microfiber is one of the highest-leverage categories to buy in volume. Per-towel cost drops dramatically at 12-pack, 24-pack, and case quantities — and unlike chemistry, microfiber doesn't expire on the shelf. If you're running a shop or mobile setup, set a recurring restock at the case level and you'll never run out mid-job.
